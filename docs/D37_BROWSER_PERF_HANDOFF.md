## Verified changes and limits

- `12e9533` indexes NPC building obstacle queries and avoids inactive water spans. `cee346a` gives up to eight nearby characters exact visual foot IK and staggers ground sampling for the rest. `65954b8` keeps dry water tiles asleep until an event wakes them, limits cliff samples per tick from 240 to 96 and sends worker input updates when the state changes. This can delay distant cliff details. World commands and networking still run when input is unchanged.
- The local smoke reached a populated low-altitude Shift flight, a cave and a camp with 48 NPCs plus the player. The longer run retained construction and terrain edits through 25 minutes. Its final attempted flight at minute 25 failed the density assertion (50 of 68 samples had at least 1,000 scatter instances, below the 75% threshold). That is an open streaming or route issue. Trace biome and altitude, desired and resident tiles, queue age and evictions to distinguish them.
- In one 96-second low flight with 54,509 scatter instances at the end, rendering averaged 162 FPS with 12.1 ms p99 frames. The world executed 48.2 ticks/s and skipped 1,129 ticks. In a dense camp phase after edits it executed 18.6 ticks/s at 164.5 FPS. In that phase `~actor_animation` averaged 40.2 ms of the 50.3 ms worker tick. The earlier cave walk reached 60 ticks/s. The 30-minute acceptance, 10 simultaneous clients and a physical phone are still open.

## Automatic streaming from orbit to the player

The current `d37-planet/src/lod.rs` already has a cube-sphere quadtree, distance-based subdivision, horizon culling and neighbour stitching. `d37-app/src/planet.rs` streams meshes with ancestor fallbacks and a resident cache. Build the following on those systems.

1. **Select by what the camera can see.** A distant planet is one render proxy and one simulation summary. As its projected diameter grows, begin streaming the visible hemisphere. Refine nodes by projected geometric error in pixels, accounting for FOV and resolution. Use horizon and frustum tests for render demand, with a margin for turning. The player's collision neighbourhood has its own demand even when the camera faces away. For caves use the current interior cell and visible portal neighbours, with brief overlap on transitions and a fallback for open or destroyed walls.
2. **Open at usable local detail.** The browser now checks local spawn coverage for an ordinary ground start. For cave starts, wait for the collision and mesh of the occupied interior section and the first visible portals. For saved flight and orbit, establish separate readiness checks for the visible ground or coarse planet representation. Leave distant details and full-quality textures for later. Track time to first visible frame, first accepted input and the first settled view separately.
3. **Use one priority policy for queued work.** Order by local collision and actions, missing visible coverage, predicted movement and background refinement. Use camera velocity to prefetch a short flight corridor. Re-rank pending work after turns, teleports and stage changes. Attach a key and revision to each result, and discard results that are stale when they arrive. Cap worker jobs, upload bytes and time per frame. Keep the coarser parent until the needed children are ready. Bound RAM, persistent cache and GPU residency separately. Cache keys include seed, generation version and relevant edit revision. A missing cached mesh is regenerated from the seed and saved changes.
4. **Activate by gameplay and proximity.** Store settled objects as data with a stable ID and spatial index, independently of their room and renderer. Wake them on impact, movement, placement, support changes, nearby actions or an approaching player. Keep full physics near active players. For distant movement store a trajectory and its tick, then re-evaluate on a relevant event. Aggregate far settlements by region and advance scheduled work by elapsed time. Transfer objects between cells transactionally. Unloading a chunk or demolishing a room must preserve moved objects and their ownership.
5. **Batch data movement.** Send dirty entries and their versions instead of complete object arrays each tick. Workers prepare terrain, collider candidates and visibility data independently, then apply gameplay results in a stable order. GPU jobs handle culling, LOD selection for resident geometry, instancing, wind and particles. On the server, simulate the union of active areas for connected players and send each client the relevant acknowledged changes through the existing authority. Give shared regions one authoritative owner.
6. **Tune with the real route.** Capture queue age, bytes pending/uploaded, cancelled work, near/distant active counts, tick time, frame p95/p99 and input age. Set device-specific budgets and lower distant visual detail with hysteresis when sustained frame pressure rises. Compare cold and warm starts, repeated Shift passes through the same cells, orbit-to-ground, entering and leaving a cave, and 30 minutes with buildings, edits and NPCs. Test ten split and overlapping multiplayer interest areas. Verify that caches plateau, nearby objects remain interactable and distant coverage stays visible while details arrive.

## Factorio techniques for D37 multiplayer

[Factorio's entity budgets and aggregation](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-148) and [ordered worker wake-ups](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-364) map to D37 sleeping water tiles, settled debris, and dirty cells. Research the current wake owners per region before adding a queue. Track active versus dormant counts and wake causes to catch missed wakes after digging, a collision, editing a building or crossing a chunk boundary.

Factorio uses deterministic lockstep and a separate prediction layer. D37 already has server authority, client prediction and per-cell digests in `crates/d37-app/src/authority.rs` and `crates/d37-net/src/auth.rs`. Reuse these paths. The current digest covers placed parts, positions, open and lit state. Before changing wire behavior, add diagnostics for server tick, cell revision, last applied command sequence, local and server digest, first mismatch tick, resync requests, corrections and queue ages. Compare equivalent confirmed revisions after acknowledgements. Separate a delayed snapshot from a mismatch at the same revision. Preserve a bounded command log and paired state slices for a dev-only replay. [Factorio prediction](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-302) · [Factorio desync reports](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-188)

Introduce a versioned digest only after testing coverage. Define canonical ordering and field encodings (including building rotations and placed-part state, then terrain edits and containers). Compare hashes incrementally for dirty cells, with separate scopes for seeded terrain, building state and inventories. Exclude render caches and transient prediction. Start with a replay of identical acknowledged commands on native and WASM at different render rates. Deliberately alter one cell to verify localisation and recovery. Keep this validation separate from publishing a solo browser build.

## Server work in order

The authoritative server already targets 60 steps/s and sends cell snapshots or deltas at 10 Hz. `authhub.rs` shares a cell snapshot and digest within one replication round. `authority.rs` gives world simulation the positions of all connected players. The current overload guard narrows replication and can halve world stepping. Treat a shed transition as a diagnostic signal and report the actual TPS separately from client FPS.

| Order | Where to start | Change and acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Establish a baseline | `crates/d37-server/src/authhub.rs`, `crates/d37-app/src/authority.rs` | Instrument authority step, interest/index work, digest creation, encoding/queueing and disk separately. Report actual TPS, p95/p99 tick time, input-to-ack age, queued bytes and age per peer, heap and shed transitions. Keep instrumentation sampled. Run the same recorded actions with 1 player, 10 in one camp and 10 spread across distinct cells. |
| 2. Remove work that grows with the entire world | `authhub.rs::replicate`, `authority.rs::entities` and `digest` | Maintain entity membership by current cell and dirty revision at mutation boundaries. Diff subscribed dirty cells; reuse an encoded delta only for peers with the same acknowledged baseline. Handle creation, removal and crossing cells in the same tick. Update digests from changed records rather than serializing the whole save. Replay against the current path before replacing it. |
| 3. Bound networking and saving | `crates/d37-server/src/lib.rs`, `shards.rs`, `crates/d37-net/src/auth.rs` | Add byte and age limits to per-peer queues. Coalesce replaceable poses and resync obsolete cell deltas with a fresh snapshot. Keep command acknowledgements, inventory and critical edits ordered. Slow peers must neither delay the room nor silently lose confirmed actions. Move snapshot serialization and disk work off the hub loop with a bounded queue and immutable revisions. Test reconnect and restart after committed changes. |
| 4. Keep world time stable | `authority.rs::step`, `authhub.rs::auth_tick`, the gameplay systems under `crates/d37-app/src/game/sys/` | Coalesce player interest areas so shared cells run once. Use a fixed authoritative tick, wake near objects on relevant changes and schedule distant AI, water and settlements at a lower rate. Remove render-only work from the headless path. Move independent preparation to a bounded worker pool only after measuring it, then publish results in deterministic order at tick boundaries. Keep placement, hits and inventory authoritative. Render on the client and interpolate entity movement between snapshots. |
| 5. Exercise failures and then enable shared worlds | Existing `crates/d37-server/tests/` and `crates/d37-app/tests/net_host.rs` | Use a populated save with building, digging, moving items, NPCs and cave transitions. Run both 10-player layouts for 30 minutes, then repeat with RTT/jitter, a slow reader, reconnects and a restart. Verify zero duplicate items, no lost confirmed commands and no unexplained mismatches at equal revisions. Test signed room access and persistence recovery before changing the hub's `NET_URL`. |

The first target is stable 60 server TPS, p99 tick time within 16.67 ms at 60 Hz, zero sustained shedding and bounded queues and memory on the selected server hardware. Report p99 input-to-ack and bandwidth per peer separately for both player layouts. Split independent rooms between workers only if the single-room tick and the measured multi-room bottleneck justify it. A solo WebGPU test or a 10 Hz replication rate does not establish that a server can handle 10 players.
